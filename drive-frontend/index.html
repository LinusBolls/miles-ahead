Going somewhere anyway?

Take a nearly empty MILES and fill it up on the way. MILES pays the fuel and gives you €5 credit for your next ride.

    How the numbers work

    €5 for refuelling. Rent at the standard km rate in the MILES app, start with the tank below 25%, and fill it to 100% at a partner station in Germany. You start refuelling in the app ("Refueling", pick the pump, "Unlock nozzle"); MILES pays the fuel. Once per car and rental; the credit arrives after the ride, is valid 90 days and is used on your next trip. MILES says parking minutes while refuelling are compensated with the bonus. Sources: refuelling bonus, how refuelling works.

    How much cheaper. We take the €5 credit and subtract the unlock fee and the km from the car to the pump (plus 0.3 km to park), as if the pump were a pure detour. If the pump is on your way, you'd pay those anyway and save closer to €5. Add about 10 minutes at the pump (our guess). We assume €0.98/km and €1 unlock for S and M cars, €1.29/km and €2 unlock for vans and Teslas. MILES advertises S and M from €0.79/km, so the app may be cheaper. Street distance is estimated as 1.35 times the straight line. The list is sorted by saving minus about €0.20 per walking minute. Cars that would cost more than they save are never shown.

    €10 for charging. End a ride at the km rate with the battery at 30% or lower, plugged into a MILES partner charger, after driving more than 1 km (source). Berlin has few electric MILES left, so this is rare.

    Partner status isn't public. For fuel, Aral and TotalEnergies stations are marked likely; for charging, operators that roam on Plugsurfing. Always check the station in the MILES app before you drive.

    Data. Car positions and fuel levels come from the public MILES map, refreshed every few minutes, so a car can already be booked. "Under 25% right now" counts the fuel cars currently available to rent on that map. Stations from OpenStreetMap. Credit rules read from the MILES help center on 21 November 2026.
