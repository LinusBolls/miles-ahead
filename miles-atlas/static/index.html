Every day, 2,700 MILES cars in Berlin end up where nobody rents them for 12 h or more.

We predict which ones at the moment the ride ends, using TabPFN, and offer a nearby rider €5 credit to move the car to where demand is.

A feature we think MILES should build. Open research, prototype and whitepaper, free to use.

Cars sit in the wrong place, and relocation arrives a day late.

2,700cars a day strand: 18% of Berlin ride ends are followed by 12 hours or more without a rental.
24.6 hmedian time after the last ride before a MILES relocation trip starts. MILES runs about 180 of these a day.
~4×difference in next-rental wait by area. At 20:00 the best areas re-rent within 4.6 h, the worst take 17.3 h or more.
88%of stranded waits end in a normal booking, 0.1% in a repair. Stranded cars are fine. They are just in the wrong place.

Vans strand 5.5× as often, evening ride ends 3.5× (logistic regression, Berlin, 208k ride ends).

Three TabPFN predictions turn a ride end into one decision.

  1. At ride end

    Will it strand?

    Where should it go?

  3. Before offering

    Will it still be there?

    P(the car is still free when the rider arrives). Backs “money back if gone”.

    TabPFNoffline
  4. Gate

    Is it worth €5?

    Offer only when the expected gain beats the reward and refund risk. Pushes are budget-gated, about one per rider a day. A quiet day sends none.

Operator console (running)

MILES ops see the flagged cars ranked by value and grouped into runs for their own crews.

Two surfaces, one engine. Live today, step 1 runs on the TabPFN API for every Berlin ride end. Steps 2 and 3 are evaluated offline (results below); until they are wired in, the console picks destinations from historical demand per 1 km area.

It is running right now.

Every Berlin ride end our scraper sees is scored by the TabPFN API within a minute (60 s micro-batches). Cars worth moving land in the operator console as runs a crew can take: assign or skip, outcome tracked.

In a new city, TabPFN learns from local rides without any training.

With Berlin context and no training, TabPFN reaches 0.665 → 0.710 as local rides grow from 50 to 1,000. A GBM trained on Berlin only scores ≈0.65 in new cities; that comes from a different run, and the comparison setup is pending confirmation.

Worth €130k to €1.2M a year in Berlin. A two-week test tells which.

How many riders accept an offer is the one number we cannot know from data. It moves the answer by 4×, so we propose measuring it.

Proposed A/B test, two weeks

Randomly split flagged cars into offer and no offer. Measure acceptance, time to next rental and net € per flagged car.

Bike sharing already pays riders to move bikes before stations empty (Citi Bike Bike Angels); car sharing still waits a day — we bring that prediction to free-floating cars with TabPFN.

What this does not show.

  • No personalization. The models know cars and places, never riders.
  • Most stranded cars are on the edge of the city, a median 7.3 km from the centre. “4 min walk” holds for the inner-city slice. The volume is in the operator console.
  • The concept app runs on June 2024 replay data. The live scorer above runs on 2026 ride ends.
  • A GBM on all the data edges TabPFN on Berlin accuracy. TabPFN’s case is calibration at equal data, data efficiency and learning a new city from its first rides without training.
  • Acceptance is assumed, not measured, until the A/B test runs.
